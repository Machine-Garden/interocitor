No readable relay - less payload custody

Sometimes you do not want to have a privacy policy. So do not build a readable server.

Interocitor helps apps sync records and files through relays that can store, route, quota, and delete encrypted payloads, but cannot read them. You may still need legal privacy terms; payload custody does not have to start on your backend.

Shrink the promise

Do not promise restraint where architecture can remove access.

Once your backend has plaintext, privacy becomes a behavior promise. Interocitor moves the payload boundary to trusted devices so the relay can be useful without becoming a reader.

01

Give the server a job.

Store, route, quota, authorize, delete. These are useful jobs that do not require payload readability.

02

Do not give it the secret.

Records and files are encrypted before upload, so relay compromise is not automatic payload exposure.

03

Keep legal language honest.

Your product may still need disclosures. The difference is that payload custody does not begin on the server.

Boundary

Less custody is not zero responsibility.

Do say

The relay cannot read encrypted payloads without the key. Payload custody can stay on trusted devices.

Do not say

No privacy policy required, zero metadata, anonymous sync, breach-proof, or total invisibility.

Design for

Visible metadata, device compromise, key recovery, auth policy, and re-keying after access changes.
